PINTS
=====

PART ONE -- THE FOUR CLASSICS
----------------------------

1. PILSNER (Pils)
   Origin: Plzen, Czech Republic, 1842
   Style:  Pale, gold, dense white foam head, 4-5% ABV
   Taste: Crackling carbonation, cracker/bread malt, spicy noble hops,
          a mineral-dry finish. Bitter snap at the end, never sticky.
   Glass: Pilsner tulip or footed pilsner, 12-16 oz, straight up
   Serve:  38-42F / 3-5C. Never serve warm; the lacing dies.
   Beer:   Czech Saaz hops, Pilsner malt. Water is 85% of the flavor.
   Match:  Fried food, grilled chicken, sausages, rich cheeses, burgers.
   The lacing test: if the glass has no webbed foam lace left on the
   wall after drinking, the beer was over-carbonated or poured warm.
   Pour slowly down the side, leave the head intact.

2. DARK LAGER / BOHEMIAN (Bock, Schwarzbier)
   Origin: Bohemia, later Bavaria
   Style:  Deep brown to black, 4.5-6.5% ABV
   Taste:  Roasted malt, burnt coffee, cocoa, dark bread crust. Bigger
          body than a pils, drier finish than a stout.
   Glass:  Seidel or dimpled mug, 16 oz
   Serve:  45-50F / 7-10C. Pour and leave a finger of head.
   Beer:   Vienna and Munich malts, dark caramel and roasted barley.
   Match:  Sausages, bratwurst, smoked fish, hearty stews, hard cheese.
   Note:   Schwarzbier means "black beer" -- lagered, cold-fermented.

3. INDIA PALE ALE
   Origin: England 1820s, exported to India to survive the sea trip
   Style:  Deep gold to amber, 5-6.5% ABV
   Taste:  Layered hop aroma -- pine, citrus, grass, resin, tropical fruit.
          Dry bitterness, medium-light body, firm malt backbone.
   Glass:  Pint glass or tulip, 14-16 oz
   Serve:  45-55F / 7-12C
   Beer:   English hops (Fuggles, East Kent Goldings) vs. modern American
          hops (Citra, Mosaic, Simcoe). Check the label for the era.
   Match:  Burgers, spicy wings, curry, goat cheese, fish and chips.
   Trap:   If it tastes like grapefruit juice it is over-hopped to style
          trend, not to the classic recipe. Balanced is the goal.

4. STOUT
   Origin: Ireland, the porter's stevedore tradition
   Style:  Opaque black, 4-7% ABV
   Taste:  Roasted barley, coffee, dark chocolate, smoke. Creamy mouthfeel
          from nitrogen, dry roasted bitterness, no caramel sweetness.
   Glass:  Nonic pint or tulip, 12-16 oz
   Serve:  45-50F / 7-10C. Pour 3/4, settle, top the rest.
   Beer:   Pale malt, flaked barley, roasted barley, English hops.
   Match:  Oysters, mussels, barbecue brisket, blue cheese, dessert.

PART TWO -- THE MODERN SHORTLIST
-------------------------------

5. GERMAN HEFEWEISSEN
   Cloudy, unfiltered, banana and clove yeast character. 5.5% ABV.
   Glass: Full 16 oz weizen, never a small flute -- head needs room.
   Serve: 45-50F. Pour 1/3, swirl the yeast off the bottom, top.
   Food:  Weiss beer soup, pretzels, sausages, Thai food.

6. AMBER / RED ALE (Irish Red, Red Ale)
   Copper to amber, 4.5-6% ABV. Toffee, caramel malt, clean finish.
   Best value-for-flavor pint on any tap list. Always a crowd winner.

7. BLONDE ALE
   4-5.5% ABV. Clean, crisp, barely sweet, cracker finish. The pint
   you drink when you want a beer that does not ask anything of you.

8. MALTED MILK STOUT
   Lactose-containing stout, 4-6% ABV. Adds a sweet, silky body.
   Enjoyable but a genuinely dairy-free option is rare -- ask.

9. SMOKED BEER (Rauchbier, Oktoberfest)
   Beechwood or peat smoke layered under clean lager malt.
   The smoke is a seasoning, not the meal. 4.5-5.5% ABV.
   Food:  Grilled bratwurst, smoked sausages, aged gouda.

10. WHEAT BEER (Hefeweizen, American wheat)
    Light, citrus-y, soft, low hop, sometimes hazy. 4-5.5% ABV.
    Crowd pleaser for people who think they dislike beer.

PART THREE -- HOW TO JUDGE A PINT
--------------------------------

Color     Pale gold, amber, copper, brown, black. Name tells you little
          about flavor in modern brewing. Look for haze, head, clarity.

Aroma      Smell before drinking. Grain, fruit, hop cone, roast, smoke,
          yeast spice. A pint that smells of nothing smells wrong.

Head       1-2 fingers of persistent foam. Head means the pint is fresh,
          within the freshness window, and correctly stored.

Feel       Light and crisp (thin, snappy), medium and smooth (balanced),
          thick and creamy (roast, body). Mouthfeel is half the review.

Carbonation Sparkling and biting (high), soft and gentle (low).
          Over-carbonated beer pushes CO2 off the nose and dulls aroma.

Balance    Is sweetness balanced by bitterness? Strength balanced by
          refreshment? A good pint has no single flavor shouting.

Value      Price per pint vs. what you get. A $9 imperial stout that
          lasts an hour is cheaper than a $5 session lager.

PART FOUR -- STORAGE, SERVICE, GLASSWARE
---------------------------------------

Storage     Upright or inverted (single hop, no distortion -- vertical
            keeps carbonation and label intact). Cold, dark, constant
            temperature. Never in sunlight. Buy fresh, rotate.

Freshness   1-3 months bottled. 60-90 days is the hop-aroma cliff for
            IPA. Nitro cans 3-6 months. Stout and barleywine keep longer.
            Check the "best before," not the brew date.

Glassware   Drink from the glass the pint was poured into. Tulip and
            nonic concentrate aroma. A clean, beer-clean pint glass
            matters more than an expensive one. Never rinse with soap
            -- it kills the head.

Temperature  38-42F pils and lager / 45-55F ales and stouts / 50-60F
            high-ABV and imperial. Warmth releases aromatics; ice-cold
            kills them. Never serve a stout ice cold.

Poured Head Leave the head intact. Never top off an old pint with a new
            pour -- the old head is where the aromatics live.

Water      Still, cold, clean. Sparkling water over-foams and sterilizes
            the taste. Peat-heavy water is the single biggest flavor
            input in a classic stout.

GLASS vs CAN vs BOTTLE
Cans        Light-tight, no oxygen exposure, freshest for hoppy beer,
            stacks cold, cheaper. A can is not a quality signal.
Bottles     Same, plus you get a cold glass for free. Comeback style.
Draft       Best aroma of all, but oxygen exposure and a warm line
            (the "third pint is warm") kill it fast. Order fresh, early.
