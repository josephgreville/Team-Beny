COCKTAIL RECIPE GUIDE
=====================

PART ONE -- THE HOUSE RULES
---------------------------

Ratios      Every classic cocktail is a ratio. Memorize the ratio, not
            the recipe, and you can build anything.
            Spirit-forward    2 : 1        (spirit : sour)
            Sours            2 : 1 : 1    (spirit : sour : sweet)
            Collins/Romance  2 : 1 : 1 : 1 (spirit : sour : sweet : soda)
            Old Fashioned     2 : 1 : dash (spirit : sweet : bitters)

Ice         Big cubes, less dilution, slower melt. Use the good ice you
            would drink. Never use salty ice or ice that has wrapped
            around a bottle cap.

Dilution    Stir with a bar spoon, 20-30 rotations, ice to the rim.
            Shake hard with ice only -- dry ice and cracked ice is a
            cocktail ice machine, not a home bar.

Strain      Fine strain for a clean glass, coarse for a textured drink.
            Double-strain through a fine mesh to catch pulp and ice
            shards. Silk straining is the last 5% of polish.

Salt rim    2 parts salt to 1 part sugar is the classic proportion. Cut
            a grapefruit half, run a lime wedge on the rim, dip, shake
            the shaker twice to clear excess. Dry it, do not wipe it.

Bitters     Bitters are seasoning, not sweetness. 2-4 dashes is normal.
            A full dash is roughly 1/8 oz. If a recipe asks for more,
            it usually means it wants something else.

Ice for the bath   2 parts ice : 1 part water for chilling glassware.

Stock the bar     Citrus (real lemons and limes, bottled is a compromise),
            simple syrup, grenadine, Angostura bitters, soda water,
            cola, dry vermouth, strong blender ice, decent whiskey,
            London dry gin, aged rum, Campari, vermouth di Torino.

PART TWO -- THE FIVE CLASSIC FAMILIES
-----------------------------------

FAMILY 1: OLD WORLD / STRENGTH (stirred, spirit-forward)

1. OLD FASHIONED
    Glass:  Rocks, 2-3 large cubes
    Ice:    1 large cube
    Build:  2 oz bourbon or rye, 1/2 oz rich simple syrup (2:1), 2-3
           dashes Angostura bitters, 2-3 dashes orange bitters
    Stir:   25 rotations
    Garnish: One wide orange peel, expressed and dropped.
    Ratio:  2 : 1 : bitters. Whiskey-forward, nothing hiding.
    The original recipe was simply whiskey + sugar + bitters -- all the
    citrus and sugar in circulation then came from bitters and syrup.

2. MANHATTAN
    Glass:  Cocktail or coupe, chilled
    Ice:    Stirring ice
    Build:  2 oz rye (or bourbon), 1 oz sweet vermouth (or 2 oz dry),
           2 dashes Angostura bitters
    Stir:   30 rotations, strain
    Garnish: Cherry. Maraschino, not a bin cherry, not a luxardo
           unless you are deliberately making a different drink.
    Variants: Perfect Manhattan = equal parts dry and sweet vermouth.
           Rob Roy = Scotch instead of rye.

3. NEGRONI
    Glass:  Rocks, large cube
    Build:  1 oz gin, 1 oz Campari, 1 oz sweet vermouth
    Stir:   25 rotations
    Garnish: Orange peel
    Equal parts. No recipe. The ratio is the drink.
    Variants: Americano = soda and orange, gin optional.
              Boulevardier = whiskey replacing gin.

4. MARTINI
    Glass:  Martini or coupe, well chilled
    Ratio:  5:1 or 4:1 gin to dry vermouth. Up to 2:1 (contemporary)
    Shake:  12-15 seconds hard with ice -- this is the only classic
           that is shaken, because the vermouth emulsion is the point.
    Strain: Fine strain, no ice
    Garnish: Lemon twist, or three olive skewered for dirty-style
           olive brine. Never both.
    Frozen Martini = blended with ice, "up" in the glass, crinkle or
           none depending on how long you blend it.

5. MANHATTAN DRY / SAZERAC
    Sazerac, glass: chilled rocks, no ice
    Build:  2 oz rye, 1/4 oz sugar cube, 3 dashes Peychaud's
           bitters, 2 dashes absinthe (rinse the glass)
    Stir:   30 rotations
    Method: Roll a lemon peel over the surface, squeeze to release
           oils, drop in. Discard the lemon.
    The only classic built by muddling a sugar cube with bitters.

FAMILY 2: THE SOURS (citrus and sweet, balanced, shaken)

6. DAIQUIRI
    Glass:  Chilled coupe. NEVER ice in the glass.
    Ratio:  2 oz white rum, 3/4 oz lime juice, 3/4 oz simple syrup
    Shake:  12 seconds hard
    Strain: Fine
    Garnish: None. Any garnish is a different drink.
    The lime juice is the ingredient. If it is sweet, it is a
    pineapple daiquiri and you should call it one.
    Daiquiri is the rum drink that proves a bartender can make three
    things from a fridge.

7. MARGARITA
    Glass:  Chilled coupe or salt-rimmed rocks
    Ratio:  2 oz blanco tequila, 3/4 oz lime, 3/4 oz orange liqueur,
           optional 1/2 oz agave nectar
    Shake:  12 seconds
    Variants: Tommy Margarita = omit orange liqueur.
              Cadiz = mezcal, rocks glass, no salt.
              Salt on the rim only; sugar rim = sweet and wrong.

8. WHISKEY SOUR
    Glass:  Rocks with ice, or coupe up with no ice
    Build:  2 oz bourbon, 3/4 oz lemon, 3/4 oz simple syrup,
           3 dashes Peychaud's bitters
    Shake:  12 seconds -- hold back half the foam with a fine strain
    Garnish: Lemon coin, or 2-3 drops of bitters in the foam (a "heart")
    Variants: Amaretto sour = amaretto float.

9. GIMLET
    Ratio:  2 oz gin, 3/4 oz lime, 3/4 oz simple syrup
    Shake:  12 seconds, fine strain, coupe
    Elegant, dry, sour, and about 100 years simpler than most drinks.

10. NEW YORK SOUR
    Glass:  Rocks, no ice (or a large cube)
    Build:  2 oz rye, 3/4 oz lemon, 1/2 oz sugar, 1/2 oz red wine
           float, 3 dashes Peychaud's
    Shake:  Everything except the wine, 12 seconds
    Float:  Pour the wine slowly down the side of a bar spoon
    The egg white version: 3/4 oz aquafaba or egg white, dry shake
           first to emulsify, then shake with ice.

FAMILY 3: HIGHBALLS (long, cold, built in the glass)

11. HIGHBALL
    Glass:  Collins or highball, ice to the top, tall and narrow
    Build:  2 oz spirit, 4-6 oz soda or mixer, built over ice,
           stir once with a bar spoon to lift the spirit
    Note:   Tall narrow glass keeps carbonation. A tumbler goes flat.
    The single most useful drink template in the world: pick a spirit,
    pick a mixer, 2 to 4 oz spirit, 4 to 6 oz mixer, ice.

12. GIN & TONIC
    2 oz London dry gin, 4 oz tonic, ice, one lime wedge
    Garnish: Lime wheel, or cucumber ribbon for a gin botanical blend
    Quality tonic has real quinine bite and visible bubbles. Cheap
    flat tonic is the only real failure point.

13. MOSCOW MULE
    Glass:  Copper mug (a tall glass works if you care less)
    Build:  2 oz vodka, 3/4 oz lime, ginger beer to the top
    Topping:  Float 1 oz dark rum or a splash of crème de cassis for
           color -- this is not traditional, so do it quietly.

14. DARK 'N' STORMY
    Replace vodka with 2 oz rum. That is the entire difference between
    a Moscow Mule and a Dark 'n' Stormy. Trademark issue, not
    flavor issue. The ginger beer floats because of the rum's density.

15. TOM COLLINS
    2 oz gin, 3/4 oz lemon, 3/4 oz simple syrup, 3 oz soda water
    Tall Collins glass, ice, lemon wheel. A gin sour lengthened
    with soda. Built in the glass, not the shaker.

16. WHISKEY HIGHBALL
    2 oz bourbon, 4-6 oz soda water, ice, orange or lemon twist
    Over ice is mandatory. Up, with no ice, is a different drink.

PART FOUR -- MODERN CLASSES
---------------------------

17. OLD FASHIONED, SPECS
     Float 2-3 dashes of bitters on the surface, do not stir them in.
     The bitters sit on the top and you taste them first and last.

18. ESPRESSO MARTINI
     Glass:  Nick & Nora or coupe
     Build:  1.5 oz vodka, 1 oz espresso (cooled!), 1/2 oz coffee
            liqueur, 1/4 oz simple syrup
     Shake:  10 seconds, hard
     Garnish: 3 coffee beans
     Warm espresso will melt the ice in three seconds. Cool it first.
     The classic ratios are 2 oz vodka to 1 oz espresso. Taste the
     crema before shaking.

19. PAPER PLANE
     Glass:  Coupette, no ice
     Build:  Equal parts bourbon, Aperol, amaro nonino, lemon juice
     Shake:  8 seconds
     Garnish: None, or a dehydrated lemon wheel
     All four ingredients are equal. An amaro-forward Old Fashioned
     riff that lives in the sour family.

20. CORONA MARGARITA
     Glass:  Rocks, salt rim, large cube
     Build:  1.5 oz tequila, 3/4 oz lime, 3/4 oz honey syrup (or 1 oz
            pineapple juice), 2 oz corona
     Shake:  Everything but the beer, 10 seconds
     Top:    2 oz cold corona poured down the side of a jigger
     Never put beer in the shaker -- the carbonation dies and the
     drink goes flat and warm.

21. BLUEBERRY LEMONADE
     Glass:  Collins, over ice
     Build:  2 oz gin or vodka, 3/4 oz lemon, 3/4 oz simple syrup,
            3/4 oz blueberry puree, splash of soda
     Shake:  Sugar and lemon and puree first, 8 seconds, then top
            with soda in the glass.

22. GOLDEN MILK / TURMERIC LATTE COCKTAIL
     Glass:  Rocks or mug
     Build:  2 oz rum, 1 oz honey syrup, 1/2 oz lemon, 2 oz oat milk,
            1/4 tsp turmeric, pinch of black pepper (the pepper is
            not optional -- it is how the turmeric absorbs)
     Heat:  Warm the milk and spices, do not boil.

23. PAPAYA MEZCAL PUNCH
     Glass:  Goblet or rocks
     Build:  2 oz mezcal, 1 oz papaya puree, 3/4 oz lime, 3/4 oz
            honey, 1 oz soda
     Shake:  10 seconds, pour over crushed ice
     Garnish: Tajin or chile salt rim, dehydrated chile

24. SEASONAL
     Cucumber basil gin, blood orange whiskey, rosemary rum,
     cranberry vodka, ginger-lemon bourbon, honey-fig amaro drinks.
     The rule: seasonal cocktails still obey a ratio. Use it.

PART FIVE -- HOW TO BUILD A DRINK YOURSELF
------------------------------------------

Ice to fill     Fill the shaker 2/3 with ice.
Order          Spirits first, then citrus, then sugar, then anything
               carbonated last. Citrus before sugar prevents
               unnecessary dilution.
Shake          10-15 seconds. Not 5, not 30. Your arms will know.
Strain         Fine strain for a clear drink. Always pour through.
Dilution       A properly built drink is 20-25% water by volume from
               shaking. That dilution is what makes it soft.

For a party    Punch bowl: 1 part spirit, 3 parts juice, 1 part citrus,
               4 parts water or soda, ice in the bowl to keep it cold.
               Never serve a party in the shaker.

Zero-proof     Non-alcoholic aperitif amaro, verjus, or NA spirits are
               now good enough that nobody has to drink a mocktail.
               Use 0-proof amaro, 1 oz NA spirit, 3/4 oz verjus.

Glasses            Martini/coupe for sours (no ice, chilled glass),
                    rocks for spirit-forward (big cube), Collins/highball
                    for anything long, wine glass for aromatized drinks.

Garnish and what it does
       Citrus peel  Express the oils over the surface. Aromatics.
       Coin         A visual cue and a squeeze of juice on the rim.
       Cherry       Sweetness and tradition. Manners.
       Skewer       Say it is a martini instead of a dirty martini.
       Bitters      Grape brandies, amaro, orange liqueur, mint.
